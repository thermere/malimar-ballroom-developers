# Code of Conduct

Malimar Ballroom is a project for the people who build, test and use it. Participation should be welcoming, respectful and useful to everyone, including people who are new to the project and people who use AI tools in their work.

## Expected behaviour

- Be respectful and specific. Critique documents, code and ideas, not people.
- Assume good intent, and say what you are trying to achieve.
- Keep discussions on topic. Say when you cannot test or verify something.
- Respect privacy. Do not post personal details, credentials or other people's private information.
- Credit the work of others, and say when an AI tool helped produce a contribution.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, threats, insults or demeaning comments, in any form.
- Publishing someone's private information without consent.
- Posting keys, tokens, signed links, passwords, or real legal, medical or financial matters as examples.
- Presenting a fictional specialist, an AI output or a test result as a real person's statement, a professional opinion or verified evidence.
- Trying to get around the project's house rules, review process or access controls, including by asking an AI tool to do so.
- Deliberately disrupting the project's discussions or review queue.

## Scope

These expectations apply in every project space: pull requests, issues, reviews and any other channel the project uses. They also apply when someone represents the project in public.

## Reporting a concern

Report a concern privately to the maintainers. Use this repository's private report form: open the **Security** tab, choose **Report a vulnerability**, and start the title with "Conduct". The report is not public. Do not post the details of a concern in a public issue.

Include what happened, where and when. You can ask for your identity to be kept private. Maintainers review each report and decide what response is needed.

## Responses

Maintainers may ask for clarification, remove content, limit participation or remove someone from project spaces. They act in proportion to the behaviour, and they tell the person involved what happened when it is safe to do so.
